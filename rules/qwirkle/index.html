Qwirkle rules and scoring

How lines are built, how every turn is scored, the 12-point Qwirkle, trading tiles and the end-game bonus — plus a printable score sheet.

  • Building lines
  • A turn
  • Scoring
  • Ending
  • Score sheet

108 tiles: six colors, six shapes, three of every combination. Take turns adding tiles to a shared grid, building lines where every tile shares a color or a shape. You score every line you build or extend, and completing a line of six — a Qwirkle — is worth 12. When the bag is empty and someone plays their last tile, highest score wins.

Building lines

  • A line is all one color in different shapes, or all one shape in different colors.
  • No tile can appear twice in the same line, so a line can never be longer than six.
  • Every tile you place must fit every line it touches — across and down.

Taking a turn

  • Setup: everyone draws six tiles from the bag and keeps them hidden.
  • First turn: the player who can play the most tiles sharing one color or one shape (no duplicates) goes first, and scores them.
  • Play: place one or more tiles, all in a single line. They must connect to the tiles already on the table, but needn't touch each other — as long as the finished line has no gaps.
  • Or trade: instead of playing, put any number of tiles back in the bag and draw replacements. That's your whole turn, and you score nothing.
  • After playing, draw back up to six.

How it ends

Once the bag is empty, players carry on without drawing. The first player to play their last tile ends the game and scores 6 bonus points on top of that turn. Highest total wins.

Quick answers

Is a Qwirkle 6 or 12 points?

12 — six for the tiles in the line plus a six-point bonus.

Can you play tiles in two directions in one turn?

No. Everything you place in a turn goes in one line, though each tile also scores any line it crosses.

Quick-add for the big turns.

Tap +12 for a Qwirkle, +5, +1 — most turns are a couple of taps — and Tallero adds the going-out bonus when the last tile is played.
