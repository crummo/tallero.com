Farkle rules and scoring

What every roll is worth, how a turn works, and the variants tables argue about — plus a printable score sheet.

  • Scoring chart
  • Taking a turn
  • Winning
  • Variants
  • Score sheet

Roll six dice, set aside the ones that score, and choose: roll what's left to build the turn higher, or bank what you've got. Roll nothing that scores and you Farkle — the entire turn's points vanish. First past 10,000 doesn't end it: everyone else gets one last turn to catch you.

You need six dice, two or more players, and something to keep score with.

On their own, 2s, 3s, 4s and 6s are worth nothing — they only score as part of a combination.

Taking a turn

  • Roll all six dice. Every roll, you must set aside at least one scoring die. If you can't, you've Farkled and lose everything you'd built up this turn.
  • Dice you've set aside are locked for the turn — you can't re-roll them to improve a combination.
  • After setting dice aside, either roll the rest or bank: stop and add the turn's points to your score. Points are only yours once you bank them.
  • Hot dice: score with all six dice and you can pick them all back up and keep rolling the same turn. Everything you've built is still at risk — Farkle and it's all gone.

Example turn

You roll 152246. Set aside the 1 and the 5 for 150, then roll the other four: 3336. Three 3s is 300, so you're on 450 with one die left. Bank the 450, or roll that last die — a 1 or a 5 gives you hot dice, anything else is a Farkle and you score nothing.

Winning

The first player to pass 10,000 doesn't win on the spot. Everyone else gets one more turn to beat them, and the highest total when that lap finishes takes the game — so a big last turn can steal it.

Common variants

Farkle is a folk game and printings genuinely disagree. Settle these before the first roll:

  • Three 1s = 300 instead of 1,000 — the most common divergence you'll meet at a table.
  • Straight = 2,500 at some tables, rather than 1,500.
  • Four, five and six of a kind double up — each extra die doubles the three-of-a-kind value — instead of the flat 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000.
  • Getting on the board: you must bank at least 500 in one turn before any of your points count.
  • Three Farkles in a row costs you 1,000 points.
  • Shorter game: play to 5,000 for a quicker night.

Quick answers

What does “Farkle” mean in the game?

A roll with no scoring dice in it. It ends your turn and wipes out every point you'd built up that turn, though your banked score is safe.

Can you re-roll dice you've already set aside?

No. Once a die is set aside it's locked until your turn ends — unless all six score, which gives you hot dice and a fresh roll of all six.

Do you have to keep every scoring die?

No — you only have to keep at least one. Leaving a lone 5 in the roll gives you more dice to throw next time.
